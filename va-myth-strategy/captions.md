## Caption 3 — Myth #3: "I Already Used My VA Loan — It's Gone"

**[HOOK]**
You bought your first house with a VA loan.
Now you're moving, upgrading, or starting over.

And you're planning to go conventional because you think the VA loan was a one-time deal.

It's not.

**[THE MYTH]**
*"I used it already. You only get one."*

This is one of the most expensive misunderstandings in veteran homeownership.
We meet veterans every month who've been paying PMI and making down payments for years — on a loan they never needed to take.

**[THE TRUTH]**
VA loan entitlement is reusable.

Sell your home, pay off your VA loan, and your entitlement is fully restored.
Still have an active VA loan? You may qualify for a second purchase using second-tier entitlement.

Veterans have used their VA benefit 3, 4, even 5 times.
There is no expiration. There is no lifetime limit. There is no "used up."

**[WHY IT MATTERS TO YOU]**
If you're planning your next move — whether it's a PCS, a growing family, or just a new chapter — you may not need to bring a single dollar to closing.

Check before you assume.

## Caption 4 — Myth #6: "Guard and Reserve Members Don't Qualify"

**[HOOK]**
You serve every weekend.
You've deployed.
You've sacrificed time, income, and time with your family.

And someone told you the VA loan isn't for you because you're "just" National Guard.

That's not just wrong. It's a disservice.

**[THE MYTH]**
*"I'm Guard/Reserve — that's not the same as active duty. I don't qualify."*

This is one of the most underreported eligibility gaps in the VA loan space.
Thousands of Guard and Reserve members walk away from a benefit they've already earned — because no one ever told them the rules.

**[THE TRUTH]**
National Guard and Reserve members can qualify for the VA home loan benefit.

✅ 6 years of service with honorable discharge = eligible
✅ 90+ days of active duty under Title 10 federal orders = eligible
✅ Deployed during a federally recognized period = likely eligible

Your service counts.
Your sacrifice counts.
Your benefit is real.

## Caption 5 — Myth #7: "Surviving Spouses Can't Use the VA Loan"

**[HOOK]**
She asked three lenders.
All three told her the same thing:

*"The VA loan was your husband's benefit. It doesn't transfer."*

All three were wrong.

**[THE MYTH]**
*"My spouse passed away after serving. His VA benefits died with him."*

This might be the most painful myth we bust at our workshops.
Because the people affected are already carrying enough.

**[THE TRUTH]**
Unremarried surviving spouses of veterans who died in service — or from a service-connected disability — may be fully eligible for the VA home loan benefit.

In their own name.
With full zero-down access.
With no need to co-borrow with anyone.

This isn't a loophole. It's federal law.
It exists because Congress recognized that military loss should not also mean losing the path to homeownership.

**[WHY IT MATTERS]**

Call a VA-specialized lender.
Come to a workshop where someone actually knows the rules.

You may have already earned this.
