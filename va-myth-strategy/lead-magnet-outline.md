## LIE #1 — "You Need to Save a Down Payment Before You Can Buy"

**Section Header:** Lie #1 of 7

**The Lie:**
*"I'm not ready yet. I need to save at least 10% before I even think about applying."*

**The Truth:**
The VA home loan allows eligible veterans, active duty service members, and qualifying surviving spouses to purchase a home with zero down payment — no exceptions, no minimums. This is one of the most powerful financial benefits tied to military service, and it's written into federal law. You don't earn it by saving. You already earned it by serving.

**Real-World Scenario:**
Marcus is an Army vet in the Central Valley. He's been renting for three years, diligently saving. He has $22,000 in the bank and figures he's "almost ready." A colleague mentions the VA loan at a weekend barbecue. Marcus registers for a free workshop, learns he could have bought two years ago. In those two years, the median home price in his area rose $48,000. He didn't save his way to readiness — he waited his way out of $48,000 in equity.

**Action Step:**
Pull your DD-214 and check your discharge status. Then contact a VA-approved lender or attend a free VA Homebuyer Workshop to request your Certificate of Eligibility (COE). If you're on active duty, your commanding officer can provide a Statement of Service. You may be closer to ready than you think.

**Design Notes:**
- Icon: Dollar sign with a red X, or a "ZERO" graphic in bold
- Accent color callout box: "The VA loan doesn't require a down payment — that's not a loophole, that's the benefit."
- Visual: Before/After timeline showing "Waiting to save" vs. "Buying now" equity comparison

## LIE #2 — "VA Loans Take Too Long and Sellers Won't Accept Your Offer"

**Section Header:** Lie #2 of 7

**The Lie:**
*"My agent told me to go conventional. Sellers reject VA offers because they take forever and have too many requirements."*

**The Truth:**
VA loans close in 40–50 days on average — comparable to conventional and FHA loans. The "VA is slow" reputation stems almost entirely from lenders who don't specialize in VA financing, not from the loan program itself. An experienced VA lender with a competent team can close in 21–30 days. The right lender changes the outcome.

**Real-World Scenario:**
Jasmine is a Marine Corps veteran house-hunting in the suburbs. Her real estate agent, who primarily works with conventional buyers, steers her toward a 3.5% down FHA loan, warning her that "sellers in this market don't want VA offers." Jasmine follows the advice. She closes — but pays $14,000 down and picks up PMI at $220/month. A fellow Marine in the same market, working with a VA-specialized lender, closes in 28 days on a VA loan with zero down. Same market. Different lender. Completely different outcome.

**Action Step:**
Before your next offer, interview your lender specifically about their VA loan experience and average closing time. Ask: "What's your average VA closing timeline?" If they hesitate or give a vague answer, find a lender who specializes in VA loans. The loan program isn't the problem — the team behind it is.

**Design Notes:**
- Icon: Calendar with a checkmark, or a stopwatch graphic
- Split-panel visual: "Wrong lender" (long timeline, rejected offer) vs. "Right lender" (fast close, accepted offer)
- Pull quote: "VA loans don't lose deals — unprepared buyers with the wrong lender do."

## LIE #3 — "Guard and Reserve Members Don't Qualify"

**Section Header:** Lie #3 of 7

**The Lie:**
*"I'm National Guard. That's not regular military — I was told I don't qualify for the VA loan."*

**The Truth:**
National Guard and Reserve members do qualify for the VA home loan benefit — under specific service requirements. Generally: 6 years of service with an honorable discharge, or 90+ days of active duty under Title 10 federal orders during a qualifying period, makes a Guard or Reserve member eligible. Post-9/11 deployments have created additional pathways. This is one of the most underserved segments in VA homeownership — not because the benefit doesn't exist, but because no one is telling these veterans about it.

**Real-World Scenario:**
David has been in the California Army National Guard for nine years. He deployed twice under Title 10 federal orders. He has an honorable record and stable civilian income. But when he asked a bank about a VA loan, he was told "that's for active duty military." He spent the next four years renting and saving for a conventional loan down payment. At a VA homebuyer workshop, he learns he's been eligible for years. He gets his COE within a week. He closes on his first home shortly after.

**Action Step:**
If you have 6+ years of Guard or Reserve service, or deployed on Title 10 federal orders, apply for your Certificate of Eligibility through the VA's eBenefits portal or by submitting VA Form 26-1880. Bring your NGB Form 22 (National Guard discharge papers) or your Reserve discharge documents. Don't disqualify yourself — verify.

**Design Notes:**
- Icon: Shield with Guard/Reserve insignia style, or a National Guard emblem outline
- Color callout: "6 years of service. 0 reasons you shouldn't qualify."
- Visual: Checklist of Guard/Reserve eligibility paths (clearly formatted, easy to scan)

## LIE #4 — "The VA Funding Fee Makes It Not Worth It"

**Section Header:** Lie #4 of 7

**The Lie:**
*"Someone told me the VA Funding Fee is like paying a down payment anyway — so what's the point of using the VA loan?"*

**The Truth:**
The VA Funding Fee is a one-time cost — typically 2.15% for first-time use — that can be rolled into the loan balance. Compare that to PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) on a conventional loan with less than 20% down, which runs 0.5–1.5% of the loan amount every single year until you reach 20% equity. Over a 5-year hold, the VA loan almost always wins — often by thousands of dollars. And veterans with a service-connected disability rating of 10% or higher pay zero funding fee.

**Real-World Scenario:**
Two veterans, same $400,000 purchase price. Veteran A goes conventional with 5% down ($20,000), picks up PMI at $230/month. Over 5 years, she pays $13,800 in PMI before reaching 20% equity. Veteran B uses the VA loan — zero down, rolls in a $8,600 funding fee. After 5 years, Veteran B is $5,200 ahead — and never paid a dime in PMI. Veteran C has a 20% disability rating: no funding fee, zero down, and zero PMI. She's $20,000+ ahead of Veteran A from day one.

**Action Step:**
Check your VA disability rating. If you have a service-connected disability rated at 10% or higher, you are exempt from the VA Funding Fee — confirm this with your lender and make sure it's reflected on your loan estimate. If you don't have a rating, run a real side-by-side comparison with a VA specialist using your actual loan amount.

**Design Notes:**
- Icon: Scale/balance graphic — "One-time fee" vs. "Monthly PMI forever"
- Year-by-year comparison chart: VA loan vs. Conventional with PMI (simple, clean bar graph)
- Red callout box: "Disabled vet? You may owe $0 in funding fees. Verify before you close."

## LIE #5 — "VA Loan Limits Are Too Low for California Home Prices"

**Section Header:** Lie #5 of 7

**The Lie:**
*"I heard there's a cap on how much you can borrow with a VA loan. That cap is way too low for what homes cost here."*

**The Truth:**
As of January 1, 2020, VA loan limits were eliminated for veterans with full entitlement. There is no maximum purchase price limit. A veteran with full entitlement can purchase a $800,000 home in the Central Valley, a $1.2 million home in Sacramento, or a $2 million home anywhere else in California — on a VA loan, with zero down — as long as they qualify financially. The Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act of 2019 made this change permanent.

**Real-World Scenario:**
Elena is an Air Force veteran based in Fresno, California. She's been house-hunting in the $550,000–$650,000 range — well above what she assumed the VA loan covered. A lender quoted her an outdated county limit and told her she'd need a jumbo loan with 10% down. She came to a VA Homebuyer Workshop, learned about the 2020 change, found a VA-specialized lender, and purchased at $615,000 with zero down payment. The outdated information almost cost her $61,500.

**Action Step:**
If you have an existing VA loan, check whether you have remaining second-tier entitlement — county-level limits may still apply in your case. If your prior VA loan is paid off, you likely have full entitlement and no purchase price limit. Ask your lender specifically: "Do I have full or partial entitlement?" Demand a clear answer.

**Design Notes:**
- Icon: Map of California with a "No Limit" badge
- Visual: Crossed-out old loan limit figure + "Eliminated 2020" stamp
- Callout: "Central Valley. Inland Empire. Bay Area. No cap. No down payment. Just your service."

## LIE #6 — "Surviving Spouses Can't Use the VA Loan"

**Section Header:** Lie #6 of 7

**The Lie:**
*"My husband passed away after his service. I was told his VA benefits died with him."*

**The Truth:**
Unremarried surviving spouses of veterans who died in service, or as a result of a service-connected disability, may be fully eligible for the VA home loan benefit — in their own name. This is not a derivative benefit or a workaround. It is a standalone federal benefit extended specifically to surviving spouses, recognizing that military loss should not also mean losing access to homeownership. Some surviving spouses who have since remarried may also retain eligibility under specific circumstances.

**Real-World Scenario:**
Patricia's husband, a Marine Corps veteran, passed away from a service-connected illness two years after his discharge. Patricia, then in her 40s, assumed any military benefits she might have had were tied to her husband's active status and were now gone. Three different lenders confirmed this — all three were wrong. A VA Homebuyer Workshop outreach coordinator connected her with a VA specialist who helped her obtain a Certificate of Eligibility. Patricia purchased a home — in her name, with zero down — less than 90 days later. She has been a vocal advocate for surviving spouse outreach ever since.

**Action Step:**
If your spouse died in the line of duty or from a service-connected condition, contact the VA directly or attend a VA homebuyer workshop to verify your eligibility. You'll need VA Form 26-1817 (Request for Determination of Loan Guaranty Eligibility — Unmarried Surviving Spouses) and supporting documentation. Don't rely on the word of a general lender — get a second opinion from someone who knows VA surviving spouse eligibility cold.

- Tone guidance for designer: This section should feel warm and empowering, not somber. Use softer color accent (cream, gold, or sage green rather than red or bold navy)

## LIE #7 — "My Credit Score Is Too Low — I Don't Qualify"

**Section Header:** Lie #7 of 7

**The Lie:**
*"I've got some credit issues from when I transitioned out. My score is too low. There's no way I get approved."*

**The Truth:**
The VA itself sets no minimum credit score requirement. Individual lenders set overlays, typically 580–620, which are far lower than conventional loan thresholds. Some VA-specialized lenders work with scores as low as 550. More importantly, VA underwriters are trained to evaluate the full credit picture — including context for medical debt, deployment-related financial disruption, and post-transition challenges. A low number is not a permanent verdict, and it's not the only number that matters.

**Real-World Scenario:**
Troy separated from the Army after six years and went through a rough financial transition — a medical situation generated significant debt, a few accounts went to collections, and his score dropped to 572. Every bank he approached for a mortgage turned him down. A friend recommended a VA Homebuyer Workshop. Troy learned that VA underwriters look at the story behind the score — that his pattern since the hardship was consistently positive, and that his income stability as a federal contractor was strong. He found a VA lender willing to work with him. Fourteen months after the workshop, he closed. His score at closing: 604.

**Action Step:**
Pull your free credit report at AnnualCreditReport.com and review it for errors. Dispute any inaccuracies — especially medical collections, which now carry less weight in mortgage underwriting. Then speak directly with a VA-specialized lender who can evaluate your full file, not just your score. Don't count yourself out before you've talked to the right person.

**Design Notes:**
- Icon: Credit score dial/gauge showing movement from "low" to "qualifying" range
- Callout box: "The VA doesn't set a credit score floor. Don't let a number stop you from making the call."
- Visual: Simple upward trend line — showing credit recovery trajectory, not a harsh number
