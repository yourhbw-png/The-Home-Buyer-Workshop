# VA Loan Myths: The 10 Most Damaging Lies Costing Veterans Their Homes

> Ranked by real-world impact — how often each myth causes a veteran to walk away from their benefit, choose a worse loan, or distrust the VA loan program entirely.

---

## Myth #1 — "I need to save up a down payment before I can buy."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Hard**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"I'm not ready yet. I need to save at least 5–10% down first before I can even think about buying."*

### The Truth
The VA loan is one of the only mortgage programs in the U.S. that allows qualified buyers to purchase with **zero down payment** — no exceptions, no minimums, no tricks. A veteran with solid income and a qualifying credit score can buy a $500,000 home without a single dollar toward a down payment. This is not a workaround — it's a congressionally mandated benefit earned through service.

### Why This Myth Exists
Civilians, family members, and even some real estate agents default to conventional loan logic — where 3–20% down is standard. Veterans hear this advice from well-meaning people who simply don't know the rules of the VA program. Some lenders also push conventional loans because they earn more on them, and never correct the assumption.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"The VA loan doesn't require a down payment — that's not a loophole, that's the benefit."*

## Myth #2 — "VA loans take forever to close. Sellers hate them."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Hard**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"My agent told me VA offers keep getting rejected because sellers don't want to deal with the hassle and the long closing times."*

### The Truth
VA loans close in **40–50 days on average** — comparable to FHA and conventional loans. The "VA loans are slow" reputation is largely outdated and often spread by listing agents who had one bad experience years ago, or who simply prefer working with conventional buyers. An experienced VA lender with a strong team can close in 21–30 days. The issue is almost always lender selection, not the loan type.

### Why This Myth Exists
During peak market frenzy (2020–2022), some VA appraisals did take longer due to appraiser shortages — a problem that affected all loan types. VA appraisers also flag required repairs that conventional loans let slide, which sellers sometimes interpret as "trouble." These frustrations got attributed to the loan program rather than to market conditions and inexperienced loan officers.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"VA loans don't lose deals — unprepared buyers with the wrong lender do."*

## Myth #3 — "I used my VA loan already, so I can't use it again."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Medium**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"We used the VA loan when we bought our first house. That's a one-time thing, right? We'd have to go conventional this time."*

### The Truth
VA loan entitlement is **reusable** — for life. Veterans can use their VA benefit multiple times, simultaneously in some cases, as long as entitlement has been restored (by selling and paying off the prior home) or they have remaining second-tier entitlement. Many veterans have used their VA loan 3, 4, even 5+ times throughout their careers. One of the most powerful features of this benefit is that it doesn't expire or run out.

### Why This Myth Exists
The language around "entitlement" is confusing and rarely explained at closing. Lenders often fail to walk veterans through restoration and reuse procedures. The word "entitlement" itself sounds like a one-time allotment, which reinforces the misconception that once used, it's gone.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"Your VA benefit doesn't have an expiration date — and it doesn't have a 'used up' date either."*

## Myth #4 — "The VA Funding Fee wipes out all the savings from not having a down payment."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Medium**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"Someone told me the funding fee is like paying a down payment anyway, so what's the point?"*

### The Truth
The VA Funding Fee for first-time use is typically **2.15% of the loan amount** — a one-time cost that can be rolled into the loan. Compare that to years of PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance) on a conventional loan with less than 20% down, which runs 0.5–1.5% **per year**. Over a 5-year hold, a VA loan buyer almost always comes out thousands of dollars ahead. And veterans with a service-connected disability rating of 10% or higher are **exempt from the fee entirely**.

### Why This Myth Exists
The funding fee sounds large in isolation. If someone hears "$6,450 fee on a $300K home" without context, it sounds punishing. What they don't hear is that the alternative — PMI on a conventional loan — costs that same amount every year until 20% equity is reached. The math is almost always in the veteran's favor.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"PMI costs you every single year. The VA Funding Fee is one time — and disabled vets don't pay it at all."*

## Myth #5 — "VA loans won't work for the homes I want to buy. The limits are too low."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Medium**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"I heard there's a cap on how much you can borrow with a VA loan. That won't cut it for California prices."*

### The Truth
As of January 1, 2020, **VA loan limits were eliminated for veterans with full entitlement**. There is no ceiling on how much a veteran can borrow — the VA will back the loan regardless of purchase price, as long as the veteran qualifies financially. In high-cost markets like the Central Valley, Bay Area, or Southern California, this is a massive advantage. Only veterans with reduced entitlement (an existing active VA loan) face county-level limits.

### Why This Myth Exists
Before the Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act of 2019, VA loans were capped at conforming loan limits — typically around $417,000–$726,000 depending on the county. That cap has been gone for years, but the myth lives on because older resources still circulate, and lenders who don't specialize in VA loans often don't know the current rules.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"VA loan limits were removed in 2020. If you qualify for the payment, there's no cap on the price."*

## Myth #6 — "Guard and Reserve members can't use the VA loan."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Hard**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"I'm National Guard. That's not the same as active duty — I've been told I don't qualify for the VA loan."*

### The Truth
National Guard and Reserve members **do qualify** for the VA home loan benefit — it just requires meeting specific service requirements. Generally, Guard/Reserve members qualify after **6 years of service** (with an honorable discharge, discharge under honorable conditions, or retirement), or after **90 days of active-duty service during a federally recognized period of war or national emergency**. Deployments under Title 10 federal orders (not state orders) also count. This is a widely misunderstood eligibility category and one of the most underserved segments.

### Why This Myth Exists
Historically, Guard and Reserve eligibility was more restricted. Rules have changed — particularly with post-9/11 deployments creating new qualification pathways — but lenders and even VA regional offices haven't always communicated those updates clearly. Many Guard/Reserve members have simply never been told they might qualify.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"If you served in the Guard or Reserve, there's a real chance you already qualify — get your Certificate of Eligibility before you assume you don't."*

## Myth #7 — "Surviving spouses can't use the VA loan."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Hard**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"My husband passed away after his service. I was told his VA benefits died with him."*

### The Truth
Surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or as a result of a service-connected disability may be **fully eligible** for the VA home loan benefit — in their own name. An unremarried surviving spouse of a veteran who died on active duty or from a service-connected disability can apply for a Certificate of Eligibility and use the VA loan just like any veteran would. Some surviving spouses who have since remarried may still qualify under certain conditions. This benefit is real, available, and criminally underutilized.

### Why This Myth Exists
Surviving spouses are rarely marketed to by the real estate or mortgage industry. The VA's own outreach to this group is minimal. Many surviving spouses don't know they have this benefit, and even when they ask, they sometimes encounter lenders who are unfamiliar with the eligibility rules and incorrectly turn them away.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"If your spouse died in service or from a service-connected condition, their VA loan benefit may still belong to you."*

## Myth #8 — "My credit score is too low. VA loans require good credit."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Easy**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"I've got some credit issues from when I got out. I figured I'd need to fix everything before I could even apply."*

### The Truth
The VA itself does not set a minimum credit score requirement. Individual lenders set their own overlays — typically 580 to 620 — which are significantly lower thresholds than conventional loans. Some VA-specialized lenders work with scores as low as 550. Beyond the score, VA underwriters are trained to look at the **full credit story** — including the context of medical debt, deployment-related financial hardship, and post-service transition challenges. A credit score is one data point, not a verdict.

### Why This Myth Exists
Credit score gatekeeping is standard messaging from banks and conventional lenders. Veterans hear "you need a 680+" so often from non-VA sources that they assume it applies across the board. Many never call a VA lender to find out the actual minimum — they self-disqualify and never apply.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"The VA doesn't set a credit score floor — and a good VA lender looks at your whole story, not just a number."*

## Myth #9 — "The VA home inspection requirements make sellers reject VA buyers."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Medium**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"The VA has all these extra inspection requirements that make sellers nervous. They'll just take a conventional offer."*

### The Truth
The VA appraisal is not a home inspection — it's a valuation that includes **Minimum Property Requirements (MPRs)** to ensure the home is safe, sound, and sanitary. These requirements (functional utilities, no exposed lead paint, adequate roof life, etc.) protect the veteran, not the lender. In practice, most move-in-ready homes pass without issues. When repairs are needed, sellers can pay for them, the buyer can request a price reduction, or the issue can be addressed before closing. VA buyers have more tools to negotiate, not fewer.

### Why This Myth Exists
Listing agents who've had a deal fall apart due to a VA appraisal repair requirement spread the story — often omitting that the house genuinely had a problem. The real estate community has amplified a handful of legitimate repair flags into a narrative that VA buyers are "trouble," when in reality they're protected buyers.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"VA Minimum Property Requirements exist to protect you — not to kill deals on homes that are actually in good shape."*

## Myth #10 — "VA loans are only for first-time homebuyers."

**Difficulty to Overcome: Easy**

### The Myth (how clients actually say it)
*"I bought a house before with a conventional loan years ago. I didn't think I could use the VA loan since I'm not a first-time buyer."*

### The Truth
The VA home loan has **no first-time buyer restriction** — never has, never will. Veterans can use their VA benefit whether it's their second, third, or fifth home purchase. The benefit is tied to military service, not buyer history. Veterans who previously purchased with conventional loans are often shocked to learn they've been leaving this benefit on the table for years.

### Why This Myth Exists
First-time buyer programs and VA loan benefits often get lumped together in educational materials, real estate websites, and even HUD resources. The association sticks. Veterans assume that because they've bought before, the "special" programs no longer apply to them.

### One-Liner Rebuttal
*"The VA loan doesn't care how many homes you've bought before — it only cares that you served."*
